I am a first-year Ph.D. student at USC. I study AI agents that evolve over time, and how to make them reliable as they change. My current questions:

  1. Evaluation. How do we tell whether an agent has actually improved, rather than just scoring well on the final answer?
  2. Generalization in dynamic environments. Can agents model how a world changes over time, and reason over long, evolving temporal context?
  3. Continual self-evolution. When an agent keeps updating itself, when does past experience help, and when does it lock in behavior that no longer fits the environment?

Background. Before agents, I worked on learning when data are incomplete or the distribution shifts: time-series imputation and forecasting (NeurIPS ’25, ICML ’26, ECML-PKDD ’25, KDD ’26), graph learning under distribution shift (TKDD ’25), LLMs for molecular discovery (ACL ’26), and incremental, multi-scenario recommendation at Tencent and Alibaba (CIKM '23, WSDM '24, WSDM '25, KDD '25).

Open to collaboration. If you work on agent evaluation, self-improving systems, or AI in changing environments, I would be glad to hear from you. Please email me at kzhang70 [at] usc [dot] edu.
